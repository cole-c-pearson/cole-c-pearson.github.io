How the fit works

Each acid i with total concentration Ci releases on average n̄i(h) protons at hydrogen-ion concentration h, computed from its pKa values. Charge balance after adding V mL of NaOH to V₀ mL of sample is:

h + CbV/(V₀+V) = Kw/h + Σi CiV₀/(V₀+V) · n̄i(h)

Rearranged, every measured point (V, pH) gives one equation that is linear in the unknown concentrations:

Σi Ci n̄i(h) = [ V(Cb + h − Kw/h) + V₀(h − Kw/h) ] / V₀

The fitter solves this system by non-negative weighted least squares. Each point is weighted by how much a 0.01 pH or 0.02 mL reading error moves it, so points near the equivalence point, where volume is known precisely, count for more than points on the flat alkaline tail. Uncertainties are standard errors scaled by the reduced χ².

Activities are taken as 1, so the pKa values act as apparent constants at your ionic strength. Two acids with nearly the same pKa produce the same curve shape, and a titration alone cannot separate them. The fitter warns you when that happens.
